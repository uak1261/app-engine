1. Co-parenting records (86/100)

  • Evidence: OurFamilyWizard earns $690K/mo, up 38% in the last 30 days. TalkingParents earns $500K/mo. Documented (Rise Advertising LLC) launched in August 2026 and already earns ~$108K/mo, with only $489 of Meta spend in the last 30 days. Both leaders earn about $20 to $28 per download.
  • Why a newcomer can win: the leader's US reviews are angry:
    • "New update RUINED this App."
    • "When you download the messages for court... the reports doesn't show all replies and... attachments."
    • The "5 GB... is a LIFETIME limit" that pushes users from $149.99/yr up to $216/yr.
    • "So expensive. So many frequent bugs."
    Many families are ordered by a court to use these apps, so they are paying customers, not fans.
  • Wedge: a single parent can document everything without the other parent signing up. Entries are tamper-proof and time-stamped, and court PDF exports always include every message and attachment. Storage is unlimited.
  • MVP in 4 weeks:
    • A journal where each entry is locked with a time and location stamp.
    • Photo and receipt capture.
    • A custody calendar that supports multiple schedules.
    • An AI-written court summary as a PDF.
    • An expense tracker with reminders.
  • Monetization: $14.99/mo or $149.99/yr, undercutting OurFamilyWizard. About 2,600 to 3,100 subscribers reach $40K/mo.
  • Acquisition: Documented shows organic and search demand works. Add Meta ads targeting recently divorced parents and partnerships with family-law attorneys.
  • Kill risks:
    • Courts may name a specific app in their orders.
    • Legal-evidence claims would need careful wording.
  • $500 smoke test: a landing page promising "Court-ready co-parenting records. Tamper-proof. No storage caps." Run Meta ads to parents aged 28 to 45 with divorce or separation interests. Pass if the cost per waitlist sign-up is under $3.

2. GLP-1 companion (84/100)

  • Evidence: 5 publishers earn $30K/mo or more.
    • MeAgain: $566K, +12%.
    • Shotsy: $361K. Revenue is up 19% over both 30 and 90 days, and monthly active users are up 43%.
    • PeptidePal: $151K.
    • Monju: $138K, +29%.
    • Pep AI: $32K.
    Shotsy charges $9.99 to $14.99/mo or $39.99 to $59.99/yr.
  • Why a newcomer can win: the market is growing, not saturated. The top reviews mainly ask for better food logging: "I usually end up putting my food into ChatGPT to get the macros." The leaders are all-in-one trackers, and none owns the protein, muscle and maintenance angle.
  • Wedge: help users keep muscle while on GLP-1, and keep the weight off after stopping. Photo-based protein logging, side-effect patterns, and a plan for tapering off.
  • MVP in 4 weeks:
    • Dose log and reminders.
    • Photo-to-protein logging with an AI vision model.
    • Side-effect journal.
    • Weight trend from Apple Health.
    • "Life after GLP-1" maintenance mode.
  • Monetization: $12.99/mo or $49.99/yr. About 3,150 monthly subscribers reach $40K/mo.
  • Acquisition: the leaders run little paid advertising, so TikTok creators and Meta ads are both open.
  • Kill risks:
    • Apple's medical-app review and any wording that sounds like medical advice. Stay strictly a tracker.
    • MeAgain and Shotsy could add photo logging quickly.
  • $500 smoke test: "On Ozempic or Zepbound? Hit your protein from a photo. Keep the muscle." Meta ads targeting GLP-1 interest audiences.

3. Crochet and knitting companion (82/100)

  • Evidence: YarnPal earns $577K/mo. Loopsy earns $140K/mo, with revenue up 39% over 30 days and 49% over 90 days. Loopsy's Meta spend is $22K over 30 days, about 16% of its revenue, so its ads pay back. WoodSense ($235K) shows the same model working for woodworking. There is a long tail of apps at ~$10-15K.
  • Why a newcomer can win: YarnPal's US reviews call its patterns "AI slop":
    • "all patterns I've tried are AI generated and have mistakes."
    • "Doesn't grow with you."
    • "Can't find previously saved patterns."
  • Wedge: patterns made by real people, verified by makers, with a revenue share for independent designers. Add accurate yarn-quantity estimates and levels from beginner to advanced.
  • MVP in 4 weeks:
    • Row and stitch counter.
    • Pattern library from 20 or more paid designers.
    • Project tracker.
    • Yarn stash and quantity calculator.
    • An AI helper that explains steps but does not generate patterns.
  • Monetization: $4.99/wk or $39.99/yr, plus a $99.99 lifetime plan as Loopsy offers. About 2,200 weekly subscribers, or a mix with annual plans, reach $40K/mo.
  • Acquisition: Meta ads (proven by Loopsy), plus TikTok and Instagram maker creators.
  • Kill risks:
    • Signing up designers takes time.
    • The audience is very price-sensitive.
  • $500 smoke test: "Crochet patterns that actually work. Made by real designers, not AI." Meta ads to crochet interest audiences.

4. AI sermon notes (80/100)

  • Evidence: Bible Note earns $201K/mo at $10 per download. HolyNote launched on Sep 8, 2026 and reached $198K/mo within about 2 months, charging $9.99/wk or $59.99/yr. HolyNote's Meta spend is $5.2K over 30 days, about 3% of revenue. Bible Note's revenue fell 33% and its downloads fell 90% over 30 days, which looks like HolyNote taking its share.
  • Why a newcomer can win: Bible Note reviews complain about price and missing features:
    • "7 dollars for a weekly subscription is absolutely absurd... monthly... is not offered."
    • Notes "don't carry over" between iPad and phone.
    • The transcript comes out as "one long paragraph."
  • Wedge: fair pricing with a monthly plan, notes synced across devices, structured notes with headings, and shareable notes for small groups.
  • MVP in 4 weeks:
    • Record a sermon or import a YouTube link.
    • Structured AI notes with verses, reflection questions and a prayer.
    • iCloud sync.
    • Share to a small group.
  • Monetization: $9.99/mo or $49.99/yr. About 4,700 monthly subscribers reach $40K/mo.
  • Acquisition: Meta ads (proven by HolyNote), plus church and small-group referrals.
  • Kill risks:
    • Users push back on paying for a faith app ("This should be free").
    • Free Bible apps could add AI notes.
  • $500 smoke test: "Never forget a sermon. AI notes, synced everywhere, $9.99/month."

5. Trading card scanner and pre-grader (76/100)

  • Evidence: Digital Grading Co earns $303K/mo, with downloads up 43%. Cardly AI earns $208K/mo, with revenue up 111% over 90 days. Both earn $6.70 to $8.50 per download, and Cardly is driven mostly by organic TikTok.
  • Why a newcomer can win: Cardly's rating dropped 1.0 point in 90 days, and its reviews say:
    • "Everything is an 8... I ran a PSA 10 and it came back as an eight."
    • "Valuation is always wrong."
    • "Not one free scan to test."
  • Wedge: grading you can trust. Measure centering and corners and show the reasons behind each grade. Give 3 free scans. Pull live prices from recent sold listings, not estimates.
  • MVP in 4 to 6 weeks:
    • Card identification using a vision model plus a card database.
    • Centering measurement tool.
    • Sold-price lookup.
    • Collection value tracker.
  • Monetization: $5.99/wk or $39.99/yr. About 1,800 weekly subscribers reach $40K/mo.
  • Acquisition: organic TikTok "pull and grade" content.
  • Kill risks:
    • Accuracy is the whole product.
    • Price data needs licensing.
    This is the hardest of the 5 to build.
  • $500 smoke test: "Know if it's a 10 before you pay PSA." Run TikTok Spark Ads with creators.
